Help and About

Everything you need to run a session. For the radio calls themselves, see the Phraseology Playbook.

How to start

  1. Open the Home page and enter the passcode. It is asked once per browser tab session; scopes opened from Home do not ask again.
  2. New to it? Run the guided Intro first. Final (PAR) also has a guided intro with Aero on its start screen.
  3. Pick a position (Marshal, Approach, Final (PAR) or Instructor). Each opens in its own tab.
  4. On the start screen choose a level (Training, Normal or Rush) and press Start. A session runs about 30 minutes.
  5. Pick your name with the Controller box at the top of the scope so your time and grade go into the Log Sheet and your Dashboard.

Linking scopes

Open Marshal, Approach and Final in separate tabs of the same browser on the same computer and switch their link on at each start screen. When they are linked:

  • One Start: pressing Start on any linked scope starts all of them at the same level.
  • One Pause: Pause (the button, the P key or the Instructor) pauses and resumes every linked scope, and the 1x / 3x speed follows too.
  • One clock: all linked scopes show the same session time, counted from 0:00.
  • Shared traffic: Marshal and Approach each show the other's aircraft with a short data block, so you see the whole picture. An aircraft never shows twice during a handoff.
  • Marshal hands aircraft to Approach ("go silver"); Marshal and Approach both hand aircraft to Final ("go purple" / "go copper").
  • The Instructor connects to the linked scopes on its own; Final is always linked to it.

If a tab says "not detected", reload it and check the link switch on its start screen.

Voice and typed commands

  • Voice: hold Space (or the big Hold to Talk button), say the whole call, then let go. The pilot answers with its callsign, or "unable".
  • Typed: every scope also takes typed calls and short codes in its command box. Type ? and press Enter for that position's cheat sheet.
  • The full list of spoken calls is in the Phraseology Playbook, and every typed code is on its Shorthand tab.
  • Accepted aircraft on Marshal and Approach get a letter (A, B, …). Type the letter first to talk to it, for example A fh270.
  • Scope keys on Marshal, Approach and Instructor: point at a target and press T for a bearing / range line, T again to hold it, C to clear.

Typed handoffs

Type the aircraft's letter, then HND, then the color letter of the frequency you are handing it to:

SSilver (Approach)
RRed (Marshal)
CCopper (Final, second frequency)
PPurple (Final)
TTeal (Departure)

Automated handoffs run between a linked Marshal and Approach: A HND S on Marshal hands aircraft A to Approach, and A HND R on Approach hands it back to Marshal. For Copper, Purple and Teal, use the radio call (for example "go purple"). After HND the aircraft's data block shows HNDOFF and flashes until the other scope accepts it by typing the flashing letter (or ACPT) and Enter, or by clicking it. Type A HND again to cancel.

Scope widgets and arrow keys

  • On Marshal and Approach, drag a control (Scope range, Rings or RDV) from the side panel onto the radar. It becomes a small widget with ◀ ▶ arrows. Range steps 1 NM per arrow.
  • Click a widget to select it (gold outline). The arrow keys then change it: range by 1 NM per press, rings cycle through their steps, RDV turns on and off.
  • Esc or a click anywhere else lets go of it. Drag a widget by its name to move it; × or dragging it off the scope removes it. Positions are remembered on this computer.

Ship-turn switch

Each scope has a Ship turns switch. When on, the ship changes its recovery course during the session, so courses and patterns move with it. It is on by default for Final and off by default for Marshal and Approach. The Instructor can also turn the ship.

Browser and microphone

  • Voice needs microphone permission in the browser. Allow it when the browser asks. If you blocked it, turn it back on in the site settings (the icon at the left of the address bar).
  • Speak at a normal pace in a quiet room. If the microphone does not work, you can always type the call instead.
  • Something looks out of date? Reload the page. If that does not help, open Settings on the Home page and use Clear cache.

Training log and Dashboard

Every session is recorded on the Log Sheet: training time, level and grade per controller. The log is stored in this browser on this computer only. It is not uploaded anywhere and does not follow you to another computer on its own.

  • My Dashboard shows your progress from the same log: what to practice next, time, grades and a trend for each position, your guided-intro progress and your last 10 sessions. Pick your name at the top; Edit name renames you (and can move sessions logged as Guest to your name).
  • Move your log to another computer: on the Dashboard or the Log Sheet press Export my training log. It downloads a backup file with your sessions and intro progress. On the other computer press Import and pick that file. It is merged in: sessions already there are skipped, nothing is erased. Settings and sign-in are never in the file.
  • Use Export CSV on the Log Sheet to open the log in a spreadsheet or share it.

Report a problem

Every page has a ⚑ Report a problem button in the header (on a scope, in the top-left strip; on a small screen, the round ⚑ button in the bottom-left corner).

  1. Click it and write what happened: what you did, what you expected and what happened instead. Your name is optional.
  2. The report adds the details for you: the page, the site version, the session time and level if a scope is running, whether it is linked, your browser, screen size, the time and any recent page errors. Open Details added to the report to see exactly what is included.
  3. Click Copy report, then paste it into an email or message and send it to the site owner. Add a screenshot if you can.

The report never includes your typed or spoken commands or the passcode. While the box is open, keys go only to the box, so nothing is sent to the scope. Esc closes it.

About

AATCC Trainer is a set of browser-based radar scopes for practicing amphibious air traffic control aboard USS Wasp: Marshal, Approach, Final (PAR) and an Instructor view, with simulated pilots that answer by voice.

This is a training aid. It is not an official publication and is not for operational use. Procedures and phraseology are simplified for practice.
